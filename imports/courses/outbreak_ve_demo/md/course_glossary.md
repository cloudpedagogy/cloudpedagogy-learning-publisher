This glossary defines key terms used in the outbreak investigation and vaccine-effectiveness course. Use your browser's page-search function to locate a particular term quickly.

# A

## Attack rate

The proportion of an at-risk population that develops the disease during a specified outbreak period.

# B

## Basic reproduction number (R₀)

The average number of secondary infections generated by one typical infectious person in a fully susceptible population without control measures.

# C

## Case definition

A standard set of clinical, laboratory, person, place and time criteria used to decide whether an individual should be classified as a case for an investigation.

## Confirmed case

A person who meets the investigation's definitive confirmation criteria, commonly based on an appropriate laboratory test.

## Contact tracing

The process of identifying, assessing and managing people who may have been exposed to an infectious case.

# E

## Epidemic curve

A graph showing the number of outbreak cases by time of symptom onset, usually presented as a histogram.

# I

## Incubation period

The interval between infection or exposure to an infectious agent and the onset of symptoms.

## Index case

The first case identified by investigators in an outbreak or cluster; it is not necessarily the first person infected.

## Infectious period

The period during which an infected person can transmit the infectious agent to others.

# O

## Outbreak

The occurrence of disease cases above the number normally expected in a defined population, place and period.

# R

## Relative risk

The risk of disease in one group divided by the risk of disease in a comparison group.

# S

## Secondary attack rate

The proportion of susceptible contacts who develop disease following exposure to a primary case within an appropriate incubation period.

## Suspected case

A person who meets specified clinical or epidemiological criteria for the disease but has not yet met the criteria for confirmation.

# V

## Vaccine effectiveness

The percentage reduction in disease risk among vaccinated people compared with unvaccinated people under real-world conditions. It is commonly calculated as (1 − relative risk) × 100.
